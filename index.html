WORTH IT?

Type
Mobile app
Year
2026
Scope
UX, UI, interaction
Themes
Dark and light

Budgeting apps count money. This one counts the working hours behind it, so every price tag turns into one question: how many hours of my life is this?

WORTH IT? is built for impulsive shoppers, for salaried employees and freelancers who want to know their hourly worth, and for minimalists who want a quiet assistant, not an accounting spreadsheet.

Enter a price or scan a barcode, see the time it costs, then swipe right to buy or left to keep the hours as time saved.

The flow, ten screens →
Challenge A

Entering salary and hours is tedious.

Data entry is split into a spacious three-step onboarding. A custom numpad and sliders replace the system keyboard, so setup feels like a small interaction, not a form.

Challenge B

Decision screens get cluttered.

Once the price is in, the keyboard slides away and the background blurs, leaving only the time cost card. Category selection waits in a bottom sheet until after the swipe.

Challenge C

Budgeting feels like pressure.

The story moves from restricting spending to saving time. Serif type, generous space and a left or right swipe turn each decision into a light, almost playful moment.

02 / Concept redesign

Yapı Kredi Mobile

Type
Mobile banking
Year
2024
Scope
UX, UI

An established banking app, revisited. The brand stays; the hierarchy, the navigation and the way balances are presented get lighter and more personal.

The flow covers remote onboarding with ID and biometric steps, separate individual and corporate sign-in, a personalised return screen, and a dashboard that puts accounts, cards and recent transactions on one page.

Self-initiated concept. Not commissioned by or affiliated with Yapı Kredi.

Challenge A

Dense data pulls focus.

The bank's blue becomes a gradient backdrop, and accounts and cards sit on clean white cards so the numbers have room.

Challenge B

Two kinds of customer, one screen.

Individual and corporate sign-in are split into icon tabs. The corporate tab gets its own form with company code and user code.

Challenge C

Key features hide in the bottom bar.

The navigation is redrawn with line icons, and the most used action, JET QR, moves to the centre as a larger button.

03 / Concept redesign

Yapı Kredi Web

Type
Desktop banking
Year
2024
Scope
UX, UI
Homepage concept / scroll inside

The same thinking on a wide screen: markets, loan calculation, bill payments and campaigns on one page that still has room to breathe.

Sign-in concept

Self-initiated concept. Not commissioned by or affiliated with Yapı Kredi.

Challenge A

Desktop banking overloads the page.

The homepage is cut into horizontal modules with wide white space between markets, loans, payments and campaigns, and an organised footer at the end.

Challenge B

Sign-in pages are full of noise.

Links and adverts are cleared away. What remains is one card, an asymmetric individual and corporate tab, and the form.

Challenge C

Loan tools take you off the page.

A two-panel calculator works in place: amount and term on the left, and a blue result card on the right that updates with the instalment, rate and an apply button.

Design philosophy

01

Function and beauty, together.

A product earns its looks by working well. I aim for a balance between creativity and functionality, never one at the cost of the other.

02

Empathy is a method.

Surveys, user testing and feedback come before opinions. Designs improve through iteration with the team, not in isolation.

03

Less on the screen.

Clear hierarchy, room to breathe, one decision at a time. People should reach what they came for without cognitive overload.
